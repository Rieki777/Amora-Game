Amora / village map

The map should answer three questions in three seconds

Who runs what, what is actually alive today, and where the empty chairs are. Here is a diagnosis of the current map, six ways to draw a seat that is not a dot, and four layouts you can pick from. Everything below is interactive and built on the real Amora circle and role data.

Diagnosis

Five things the current map does to itself

The metaphor is right. Sociocracy really is circles inside circles, and spatial memory of "permaculture lives at two o'clock" is worth protecting. The execution is what hurts.

Problem 01

Seats orbit through the label

Role dots are laid on a full 360° ring starting at 12 o'clock, and the circle's name is drawn at 12 o'clock too. Seat one lands on the first letter of every name, every time. It is a guaranteed collision, not bad luck.

Problem 02

A dot carries no information

Filled or open is the only bit a dot encodes, and it does that with a 2px dash you cannot see at map zoom. Which role, who holds it, how senior, how urgent: all invisible until you click.

Problem 03

Size means nothing legible

Radius is driven by headcount and role count, so Development Circle dominates the centre and reads as the most important thing in the village. It is simply the one with the most rows.

Problem 04

Live and forming look the same

Eight councils that do not exist yet get the same visual weight as five circles doing the work today, separated only by 50% opacity. Two thirds of the map is future, and it looks like present.

Problem 05

No legend, no entry point

Nothing on the canvas says what a ring, a dot, a colour or a small amber dot means. A first-time visitor gets a beautiful diagram with no key to it.

Question 1

Six ways to draw a seat that is not a floating dot

All six are rendered on the same circle: Outreach & Growth, one seat filled and three open. Every one of them keeps the middle of the circle clear, so a name can never be covered again.

Question 3

Four ways to show the whole organisation at a glance

Same data in each. Click any circle to open it. Switch between them and notice how much faster some questions get answered.

A. Nested circles, debuggedKeeps the sociocracy picture. Three changes carry it: the centre now means something (the linking circle sits there, not whichever circle has the most rows), every name gets a reserved crown no seat may enter, and the eight forming councils become a named outer band instead of eight competing blobs.
B. The governance spineAuthority runs top to bottom, so the shape of the organisation is readable in one pass. Best view for a newcomer, an investor, or anyone asking "who decides what". Costs the circular metaphor.
C. The circle wallEvery circle as a card, filterable and sortable. Wins on the job the map is really hired for: find me somewhere to help. Scales to fifty circles without redrawing anything.
D. The governance wheelOne sunburst: village at the centre, circles as sectors, individual seats as the outer teeth. Densest of the four and the only one that shows every single seat in the village at once. Ceremonial enough for the wall of a community hall.
Recommendation

Ship A and C together, in that order

A is the hero picture and the thing people screenshot. C is the one that actually gets seats filled. They share a data shape and a detail panel, so the second one is cheap once the first is done. B is a strong third if you want a "how we govern" page. D is a poster.

Reserve a label crown in the layout function

In shared/mapLayout.ts the role ring runs a full 2π from -π/2. Start it at +40° and stop at +320°, so the top 80° of every circle belongs to text alone. One constant, and problem 01 is gone forever. The layout stays pure and deterministic, so the existing tests keep their meaning.

Turn seats into a capacity arc on the circle's own stroke

Seats stop being free-floating objects and become segments of the border. Nothing can overlap a label because nothing lives inside the circle any more. Filled segments draw solid in brand teal, open segments draw hollow with the amber dash and keep the existing pulse for open calls.

Give the centre a meaning

Place the linking circle at the centre and rank the ring by governance layer, not by radius maths. Size stops shouting. A one-line ordering rule in the layout beats any amount of colour work.

Separate live from forming, hard

Opacity 0.5 is not a status. Forming circles get a dashed stroke, a hatched fill and a small "forming" pill, and the eight councils move to a named outer band so they read as a horizon rather than as thirteen equal blobs.

Use the icons you already store

Every circle row already carries an icon and a color, and the seed files fill both in. Nothing renders them today. Drawing the glyph inside each circle adds recognition at zero data cost and makes small circles readable when the name has to shrink.

Add a legend and an open-seats counter

A four-item key under the canvas, and a single line at the top: "5 circles at work, 8 forming, 11 seats open". That sentence is the whole map for most visitors, and it gives the concierge bar something to answer.

Then build the wall as the second tab

Same payload, same detail panel, no new endpoint. It becomes the mobile view too, which retires the current accordion and gives phones a real map instead of a list.
