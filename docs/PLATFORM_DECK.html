Plate 02
The problem

Nine tools, none of which know each other.

Most projects coordinate across a chat, a spreadsheet, a shared drive, a form builder, a payment link, a calendar, a group thread, and somebody's memory. Each one works. None of them knows that the person who ran the water system for six months is the same person asking to move onto the land.

So contribution goes unrecorded, roles live in one founder's head, money arrives with no trace of what it bought, and the honest question — what has this person actually given, and what have we given back? — has no answer anyone can look up.

The gap is not features. It is that nothing holds the whole picture.

Plate 04
Value

A ledger that cannot quietly be wrong.

Recognition, credits and every village token move through one ledger with one rule: for each token, all balances sum to zero. Value is never created by a line of code — it comes out of a named source and lands in a named account, and both are visible.

Conservation
Asserted at every boot. If the books do not balance, the server refuses to start rather than serve a number it cannot stand behind.
Balances
Recomputed from the entries, never incremented. There is no counter to drift.
Every movement
Carries an idempotency key, so a retried request is the same act, not a second one.
A trade
Is exactly two legs in one transaction — both, or neither. No half-applied swap exists.
Recognition
Is earned, full stop. It can never be bought, and the code has no path to sell it.

The point is not that the software is careful. It is that when a member asks where their contribution went, someone can answer with a record instead of a recollection.

Plate 05
Authority

One answer to “who may do this?”

Permission is decided in a single place, in a fixed order, so authority can be reasoned about instead of discovered: admin, then a standing warning, then an appointed role, then an earned badge, then how far along someone is.

The order matters. A warning badge outranks a role and a stage — a person under review loses the specific thing under review and keeps everything else. Only an admin outranks a warning. Nothing else in the system is allowed to invent its own permission check.

Stages
The path from guest to co-creator, with capabilities that unlock along the way.
Roles
Appointed seats with terms — the water keeper, the kitchen steward — visible on the village map with the open ones marked.
Badges
Earned from settled contribution, or granted as honours. Earned ones can't be handed out.
Leaving
A member can go: what they are owed is enumerated and settled, their record anonymised, the ledger left intact.
Plate 06
Money

Four doors money comes in by.

All of them ride one payment path — one webhook, verified against the raw body, deduped per event, with mechanical reversals. A refund or a chargeback claws back exactly what that charge delivered, and only if it was actually delivered.

Payments
Products you define: application fees, donations, deposits and down payments, waitlist seats, recurring memberships, token packs.
Stays
Accommodation priced in credits — bought, or earned through work-exchange quests. One credit hosts one night.
Exchange
Members buy the village's own tokens from a stocked treasury. Token-to-token trading exists and stays off until a village turns it on deliberately.
Library
Donate a tool and earn credits; borrow against a deposit held in escrow. The credits are backed by what is on the shelf.

The line that does not move

Money flows in. Tokens never flow out. There is no path in this software to sell a village token back for cash, and building one is not a setting. Equity and voice live where they belong — governed elsewhere, shown here read-only, never traded.

Plate 07
The land

The land keeps its own ledger.

Trees planted, hectares under care, water retained — absolute counts, recorded by the people who walked the site, never a leaderboard. Alongside them, a snapshot of the village's vital signs frozen at the close of every lunar cycle, so a season can be compared to the one before it rather than to a feeling.

A wrong reading is withdrawn, not deleted: it stops counting, the reason is attached, and the correction stays visible. These numbers go to funders. A figure that can vanish without trace is a figure nobody outside can audit.

Plate 08
Together

Villages that can find each other.

Every deployment can publish what it needs and what it has spare, and read what peer villages publish. One project's idle excavator and another's unfilled week of masonry are the same problem twice, unsolved only because nobody could see both.

You choose exactly what leaves your instance. Federation is opt-in, and a village that shares nothing loses nothing.

Guide
An assistant that knows this village — its roles, its open seats, its stage of setup — and is grounded in regenerative organising, sociocracy, nonviolent communication and trust structures.
Weekly calls
A recording goes in; assigned work comes out. Every suggested task carries a verbatim quote and timestamp, or it is dropped.
Feedback
What breaks in your village can reach the people maintaining the platform — content only, never who said it, and switchable off.
Plate 09
Going live

The road to launch is a checklist, not a mystery.

A readiness page reads your live deployment and tells you what still stands between you and opening the doors: the keys not set, the sending domain not verified, the first founder not created, the terms of leaving not yet written. Some items the software checks itself. Some only a human can confirm, and it says so.

“Launched” is something a founder declares — never something the software decides on their behalf.

Plate 10
Honestly

What this is not.

It is not a currency scheme. Tokens here buy nights, tools and access inside one village. They are not investments, they do not leave, and the software will not help you pretend otherwise.

It is not finished. Some things are deliberately unbuilt pending legal advice — expiring member credits, for one, where the setting exists and the platform refuses to accept a value rather than promise something it will not do.

It is not hosted for you. You run it. That is the point: your database, your keys, your members' data, and a copy of the source you can read, change, or walk away with.
